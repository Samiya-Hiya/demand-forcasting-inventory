# 📦 Demand Forecasting & Inventory Optimization

> **End-to-end supply chain analytics pipeline** — from raw transaction data to actionable inventory policies using time-series forecasting, SQL analytics, and classical operations research models.

---

## 🎯 Business Problem

A German industrial parts distributor operates **5 SKUs** across **3 warehouse hubs** (Berlin, Hamburg, Munich) with **4 international suppliers**. The business faces two competing pressures:

- **Stockouts** causing lost sales and service-level failures  
- **Excess inventory** tying up capital in slow-moving stock

This project builds a data-driven framework to determine *how much* to order, *when* to order, and *how much safety stock* to hold — backed by statistical forecasting and inventory optimization theory.

## 🏗️ Methodology

### 1 · Data Layer
Synthetic but realistic dataset generated by `src/generate_data.py`:
- **3 years** of daily demand (2021-01-01 → 2023-12-31)
- Seasonal patterns (Q4 surge, summer dip) + trend growth + noise
- Supplier lead-time variability per SKU

All tables are ingested into **SQLite** and queried using standard SQL throughout the notebook.

### 2 · Exploratory Data Analysis
- Time-series trends by SKU
- Seasonal decomposition (trend + seasonal + residual components)
- Stockout heatmap (month × SKU grid)
- Supplier on-time delivery (OTD) performance

### 3 · Demand Forecasting
Three models benchmarked on a **hold-out test set (last 6 months)**:

| Model | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Holt-Winters ETS** | Additive trend + additive seasonality, damped |
| **SARIMA(1,1,1)(1,1,1,12)** | Box-Jenkins with seasonal differencing |
| **Naïve Seasonal** | Baseline: same month prior year |

**Evaluation metrics:** MAE · RMSE · MAPE

### 4 · Inventory Optimization
Classical OR models implemented from scratch in `src/inventory_models.py`:

**Economic Order Quantity (EOQ)**
$$Q^* = \sqrt{\frac{2DS}{H}}$$

**Safety Stock (statistical — accounts for demand AND lead-time variability)**
$$SS = Z_\alpha \cdot \sqrt{LT \cdot \sigma_d^2 + \bar{d}^2 \cdot \sigma_{LT}^2}$$

**Reorder Point**
$$ROP = \bar{d} \cdot LT + SS$$

Where: *D* = annual demand, *S* = ordering cost, *H* = holding cost per unit, *Z* = service-level z-score, *LT* = lead time

### 5 · ABC / Pareto Analysis
SKUs classified by cumulative revenue contribution:
- **Class A** (≤ 80% revenue) → tight control, high service levels
- **Class B** (80–95%) → moderate control
- **Class C** (> 95%) → simple periodic review

## 📈 Key Findings

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Best forecast model** | Holt-Winters ETS (lowest MAPE across 4/5 SKUs) |
| **Highest stockout risk** | SKU-002 (Electronic Control Unit) — CN supplier variability |
| **Inventory cost saving** | Applying EOQ to over-ordered SKU-003 reduces total cost ~18 % |
| **Supplier risk** | SupplierB-CN has highest average delay (4.2 days); OTD < 72 % |
| **ABC classification** | SKU-001 and SKU-005 are Class A — drive 83 % of revenue |

---

## 💡 Business Recommendations

1. **Raise safety stock for SKU-002 by ~30 %** to absorb lead-time uncertainty from overseas supplier
2. **Implement EOQ ordering for SKU-003 and SKU-004** — current order quantities exceed EOQ by 2×, inflating holding costs
3. **Dual-source SupplierB-CN volume to SupplierA-DE** until OTD performance recovers above 85 % threshold
4. **Prioritise forecasting accuracy for Class-A SKUs** — a 5 % MAPE improvement translates to ~€3 k/year safety stock reduction per SKU
